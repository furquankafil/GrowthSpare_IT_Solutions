1. Business & Requirement Analysis

A conversation — in person if you are nearby — about what your business does, who your customers are, and what the website must achieve. A restaurant and a B2B distributor get different sites because they need different outcomes.

2. Sitemap & Content Planning

We agree the exact page list and what goes on each page before design starts. You also get a content checklist — text, photos, prices, timings — so nothing stalls mid-project.

3. UI/UX Design

Homepage direction first, then interior templates — designed for your customers' phones, not for a design award jury. You review and revise before anything is built.

4. Development

Pages built responsively, forms and integrations wired up, content loaded. You see progress on a staging link throughout — no big reveal at the end.

5. SEO Setup

Titles, descriptions, headings, image labels, sitemap, and Search Console submission go in during the build — not as an afterthought once the site is live.

6. Testing

Cross-device checks, form delivery tests, speed review, and a final content proofread. Broken forms and typos are the most common embarrassments on new sites — we check both.

7. Deployment

Launch on your domain and hosting, with ownership in your name. The domain, code, and content belong to you — never accept a site you can't take with you.

8. Support & Maintenance

A handover walkthrough plus optional maintenance covering updates, backups, and small content changes. See examples of shipped work in our portfolio.

Why Businesses Need a Professional Website

Customers check online before they visit, call, or buy — even for neighbourhood businesses. When someone hears about your clinic, restaurant, or institute, the next step is almost always a Google search. What they find in that moment decides whether they contact you or your competitor: a clear, fast site with your services, prices, photos, and a WhatsApp button — or nothing at all.

A professional site also changes the quality of enquiries. Listings and social profiles bring browsers; a well-structured website pre-answers the routine questions — timings, location, price range, how to book — so the calls and messages you receive are from people already half-decided. And unlike rented reach on marketplaces or social platforms, your website is an asset you own: no commissions, no algorithm changes deciding whether customers see you.
